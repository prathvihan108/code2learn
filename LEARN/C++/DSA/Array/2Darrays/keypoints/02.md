In C++, when you perform the bitwise AND operation (`&`) between any number and 1, the result will depend on whether the number is even or odd.

If the number is even:
- Binary representation of an even number in its least significant bit (LSB) will always be 0.
- Binary representation of 1 is 000...001 (with all other bits as 0).
- Performing a bitwise AND between an even number and 1 will result in 0.
- Therefore, the output of `any_number & 1` will be 0.

If the number is odd:
- Binary representation of an odd number in its LSB will always be 1.
- Performing a bitwise AND between an odd number and 1 will result in 1 (as the LSB of 1 is 1).
- Therefore, the output of `any_number & 1` will be 1.

To summarize:
- If `any_number` is even, `any_number & 1` will be 0.
- If `any_number` is odd, `any_number & 1` will be 1.
